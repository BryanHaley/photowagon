The Basics

Home

Home is the first screen you see. It’s where you start a new image, open one, or pick up where you left off.

New file starts a blank image. Pick a Preset (Photo, Print, Art & Illustration, Web, Mobile, Film & Video) or choose Custom and set it up yourself, then tap Create. Options: Name, Units (Pixels, Inches or Centimeters), Width and Height, Resolution (pixels/inch), Colour Mode (RGB Color, Grayscale, CMYK Color, Lab Color), Colour Profile, Bit Depth (8, 16 or 32 bit), Background (White, Black, Background Color, Transparent).

Open shows the images in PhotoWagon’s own folder (On My iPhone › PhotoWagon in the Files app). Tap one to open it, or tap Browse Files… to look anywhere in Files, or Photos… to pick from your photo library. PhotoWagon opens JPEG, PNG, HEIC, JPEG XL, TIFF, PSD, WebP, GIF, camera raw files and its own layered files, among others.

Photos goes straight to your photo library.

Open (the list under the buttons) shows the images you already have open. Tap one to go back to it. Back to Workspace, under the title, also takes you back to the image you were working on.

Recovered appears only if PhotoWagon closed before you saved your work. Tap a row (or its Open) to open that copy, or tap Discard to throw it away. Home keeps the 10 newest copies and removes older ones.

Recent lists the files you opened or saved lately, with a small preview. Tap one to open it again.

Preferences is the gear at the top right. See Preferences below.

You can also drag an image from another app onto PhotoWagon to open it. Drop it on the canvas instead and it becomes a new layer.

The editing screen

In portrait, the screen has the top bar, the canvas (your image), the options bar, and the toolbar along the bottom. Turn your device sideways and the tools move to a column on the left, with the options bar along the top.

Document tabs appear under the top bar when you have more than one image open. Tap a tab to switch. A dot after the name means unsaved changes. Tap ✕ to close it, and choose Save, Don’t Save or Cancel if it has changes. On a phone held sideways, the tabs sit in the top bar.

The options bar shows the settings of the tool you picked, or buttons for what you can do right now, like Commit and Cancel while you crop or transform.

The toolbar holds the tools. Tap an icon to pick it. Icons with more than one tool show that group’s tools in the options bar, so you can pick the one you want. Touch and hold an icon to see tool names and what they do.

The status bar sits at the bottom left of the canvas. It shows the zoom and one fact about your image. Tap it to choose which fact: Document Dimensions, Layer Count or Current Tool. While you record an action, it also says so.

The top bar

Home (the house) takes you back to Home. Your open images stay open.

☰ opens the menus: File, Edit, Image, Layer, Type, Select, Filter, View, Window and Help. Quick Share as PNG… sits at the top for sharing your image fast.

Undo and Redo (the curved arrows) step back and forward through your changes.

Search Commands (the magnifying glass) finds any command by name. See below.

History (the clock) lists every step you’ve taken. Tap any step to go back to it. Steps after it turn grey, and you can tap one of those to go forward again.

View (the arrows pointing out) helps when you’ve lost your place. Options: Reset Tilt (turns the image level again), Reset Position (puts it back in the middle), Reset Zoom (back to the size that fits, keeping any tilt), Fit to Screen (fits the whole image, level and centred).

Layers (the stacked squares) opens the Layers panel.

Moving around the canvas

Pinch with two fingers to zoom in and out. Drag with two fingers to move the image around.

Twist two fingers to turn the image, like turning a sheet of paper. On a level image nothing happens until you twist past about 20°, so pinching won’t tilt it by accident. Once it’s turned, it follows your fingers right away. Use View › Reset Tilt to level it again.

Tap with two fingers to undo. Tap with three fingers to redo.

With a trackpad or mouse, scroll to move the image, and hold ⌥ or ⌘ while you scroll to zoom. Trackpad pinch and twist work too.

Colours

The colour well sits at the end of the toolbar. The front square is the foreground colour (what you paint with). The square behind it is the background colour, a second colour some tools and fills use. Tap the front square to choose a new foreground colour. Tap the back square to swap the two. To pick a colour from your image, use the Eyedropper tool.

Search Commands

Search Commands lists every command PhotoWagon has. Start typing a name, like “blur” or “crop”, and tap the one you want. Each row shows which menu it lives in. Commands that need settings ask for them first. If a command can’t run right now, it’s greyed out with a short reason in orange.

Help

Help › Manual opens this manual.

Help › Tutorial runs a short tour of 11 tips: the tools, a group’s tools, tool options, the top bar buttons and the colours. Tap Next to move on, or Skip Tutorial to stop. The tour starts by itself the first time you open an image, and once you finish or skip it, it only comes back from the Help menu. It needs an open image.

Help › Acknowledgements (also at the bottom of Settings on the Home screen) lists the open-source software inside PhotoWagon, with each licence.

Preferences

Open Preferences from the gear on Home, or from ☰ › Edit › Preferences › General….

Placing controls what happens when you add a picture to your image with Place. Options: Resize Image During Place (fits the picture to your canvas), Always Create Embedded Layers When Placing (brings it in as an embedded layer you can resize without losing detail).

File Handling covers safety copies and opening photos. Options: Save Recovery Information (how often PhotoWagon keeps a safety copy of unsaved work: Off, Every Minute, or every 5 to 60 minutes; 10 minutes to start), Open JPEG and HEIC in Develop (opens these photos in Develop first; camera raw files always do).

Apple Pencil sets how your Pencil behaves. Options: Double-Tap and Squeeze (what each does: As in Settings, Switch to Eraser, Previous Tool, Show Colors, Brush Settings, Undo or Off; Squeeze can also Show Palette and needs Apple Pencil Pro), Only Apple Pencil Paints (your finger moves the image while a painting tool is picked), Pressure (see below).

Pressure sets how hard you need to press for a full stroke. Pick Light, Normal or Firm, or drag the curve yourself: up for a lighter touch, down for a firmer one. Drag a point off the graph to remove it.

AI shows whether the AI Remove model is on your device. Options: Download Model, Cancel Download (while it downloads), Delete Model (frees about 25 MB; it downloads again next time you use AI Remove). The model runs on your device, so your photos never leave it.

The other items under ☰ › Edit › Preferences have no settings screen yet.

Apple Pencil

PhotoWagon reads your Pencil’s pressure, tilt and, on Apple Pencil Pro, barrel roll (turning the Pencil in your fingers). Brushes can use these to change size, opacity and angle as you paint; you set that up in Brush Settings. Fingers paint at full pressure.

Hover your Pencil over the screen (on iPads that support it) to see the brush outline before you touch down.

Double-tap and squeeze run the actions you chose in Preferences. As in Settings follows your iPad’s own Apple Pencil settings.

The palette appears beside your Pencil when you squeeze (with Squeeze set to Show Palette). It has the Brush, Eraser, Smudge and Eyedropper, your foreground colour, Size and Opacity sliders, and Undo and Redo. Tap anywhere else to close it.

Keyboard shortcuts

With a hardware keyboard attached, these are the most useful keys.

Files and editing: ⌘N New, ⌘O Open, ⌘S Save, ⇧⌘S Save As, ⌘W Close, ⌘Z Undo, ⇧⌘Z Redo, ⌘X Cut, ⌘C Copy, ⌘V Paste, ⌘J Layer via Copy, ⌘T Free Transform, ⌘F Search Commands, ⌘K Preferences.

Selections: ⌘A select all, ⌘D deselect, ⇧⌘I inverse, Q Quick Mask.

View: ⌘= zoom in, ⌘− zoom out, ⌘0 fit on screen, ⌘1 100%, F7 Layers. Hold Space and drag to move the image.

Tools: V Move, M Marquee, L Lasso, W selection tools, C Crop, I Eyedropper, B Brush, E Eraser, G Gradient, T Type, J repair tools, S Clone Stamp, O Dodge, P Pen, U shapes, H Hand, R Rotate View, Z Zoom. Press ⇧ with a tool’s key to step through the tools that share it.

Painting: [ and ] make the brush smaller and bigger, and ⇧[ and ⇧] make it softer and harder. Number keys set opacity (1 is 10%, 0 is 100%, or type two quickly, like 4 then 5 for 45%); add ⇧ to set flow. Hold ` to erase with the brush until you let go. ← and → turn the brush tip. Caps Lock swaps the brush outline for a small crosshair.

Colours: X swaps foreground and background, D resets them to black and white.

Finishing: Return commits a crop, transform or shape in progress, and Esc cancels it.

Tools

The tool icons run along the bottom of the screen in the toolbar (down the left side when your iPad is sideways). Most icons hold a small group of related tools, marked by a tiny triangle in the corner. Tap a group’s icon to pick its current tool and list all of its tools in the options bar (the row above the toolbar). Tap one there to pick it. Touch and hold a group’s icon for a menu of its tools with a short description of each, or touch and hold any icon in the options bar to see its name. Once you’ve picked, tap the image and the options bar goes back to showing the tool’s options.

A few things work the same with every tool:

Moving around. Pinch with two fingers to zoom, drag with two fingers to pan, and twist two fingers to turn the view (it starts turning once you twist past a small amount). Tap with two fingers to undo, and with three fingers to redo.

The loupe. When a tool places an exact point (a corner, a colour sample, a path point), a magnifier shows the pixels under your finger. It stays a moment after you lift so you can see where the point went.

Touch and hold to pick a colour. With painting tools, touch and hold the image to pick the colour under your finger as the foreground colour. With Clone Stamp and Repair Brush, touch and hold sets the source instead.

Painting on text or shapes. If you paint on a type, shape, fill or embedded layer, PhotoWagon asks whether to Rasterize it first, which turns it into plain pixels.

View tools

Hand lets you drag with one finger to move around the image. Touch and hold the group’s icon (or open its tools in the options bar) for three view shortcuts: Fit on Screen (show the whole image), Actual Size (one image pixel per screen pixel) and Straighten View (undo the view’s rotation).

Rotate View turns the view as you drag around the screen, without changing the image. Use Straighten View to set it back.

Zoom In zooms in where you tap, or zooms to fit a box you drag. Options: Scrubby Zoom (instead of a box, drag right to zoom in and left to zoom out).

Zoom Out zooms out where you tap.

Move and Artboard

Move drags the active layer around. The layer snaps to the canvas edges and centre, with guide lines showing where. Options: Auto-Select (picks the layer under your finger before moving it), Copy While Moving (moves a copy and leaves the original), Align (lines up the layer with the canvas’s Left Edges, Horizontal Centers, Right Edges, Top Edges, Vertical Centers or Bottom Edges, or with the selection if there is one), Free Transform (puts handles round the layer, see below).

Artboard makes a new artboard where you drag a box.

Free Transform

Free Transform puts a box with handles round the layer or selection. Drag inside the box to move it, drag a corner to scale it, and drag the round handle on top (or anywhere outside the box) to rotate it. The small dot in the middle is the reference point: drag it to change what the layer turns and scales around. Tap Commit (✓) to apply or Cancel (✕) to throw it away.

Options: Keep Proportions (corners scale without stretching), Reference Point (move the reference point to a corner, edge or the centre), Scale From Reference Point (scale around the reference point instead of the opposite corner), Snap Rotation to 15°, Rotate 90° Left, Rotate 90° Right, an angle box (type an exact angle), Flip Horizontal, Flip Vertical.

Marquee tools

These select regular shapes. Like all selection tools, they have three mode buttons at the start of the options bar: Replace (a new selection), Add (adds to the selection) and Subtract (takes away from it).

Rectangular Marquee selects a rectangle you drag. After you draw it, drag its edges or corners to adjust it, or drag inside to move it. A tap on the image deselects. Options: mode, Feather (softens the edge by this many pixels), Anti-alias (smooths jagged edges). While the box is showing you also get Lock Ratio and X, Y, W, H boxes to type its exact position and size.

Elliptical Marquee works the same way but selects an oval. Same options as Rectangular Marquee.

Single Row Marquee and Single Column Marquee select one row or one column of pixels across the whole image where you tap. Options: mode, Subject, All (selects the whole image).

Lasso tools

Lasso selects a freehand shape. Draw round the area with your finger and lift to close it. Options: mode, Feather, Anti-alias.

Polygonal Lasso selects a shape with straight sides. Tap each corner, then tap the first point (or tap Commit) to close it. Drag a point to move it. Options while drawing: Commit, Cancel, Undo Last Point. Before you start: mode, Feather, Anti-alias.

Magnetic Lasso follows edges for you. Trace roughly along the edge of an object and lift; the outline snaps to the edge it finds. Options: mode, Subject, All.

Object Selection, Quick Selection and Magic Wand

Object Selection finds an object for you. Tap an object, or drag a box round it. With Object Finder on, touch and hold to highlight the object under your finger, then lift to select it. Options: mode, Object Finder (highlights objects as you hold), Show All Objects (shows every object it can find), Subject (selects the main subject of the photo).

Quick Selection grows a selection as you paint over an area; it spreads to similar colours and stops at edges. Use the Subtract mode to paint parts back out. Options: mode, Size, Sample All Layers (looks at every layer, not only the active one), Enhance Edge (a smoother, cleaner edge), Subject. You can also touch and hold the group’s icon for Select Subject.

Magic Wand selects colours like the one you tap. Options: mode, Tolerance (how different a colour can be and still be picked, 0 to 255), Contiguous (only touching pixels; off picks that colour everywhere), Sample All Layers.

Select Subject (the Subject button) looks at the photo and selects the main subject, such as a person, pet or product. It uses the active layer unless Sample All Layers is on.

Every selection tool’s options also have Select All, and Paste once you’ve copied something.

When something is selected

Once there’s a selection, and you’re using a selection tool, a type tool, Move, Patch or Move and Fill, the options bar shows buttons for it instead.

Deselect removes the selection. Invert selects everything that wasn’t selected. Fill fills the selection with the foreground colour. Quick Fill deletes what’s inside and fills it from the area around it. AI Remove fills it with the on-device AI (see AI Remove below). Delete clears what’s inside to transparent. Copy copies the selected part. Paste pastes what you copied as a new layer, in this image or another open one: switch to its tab first. Select All selects the whole image. Copy to Layer copies the selected part to a new layer. Mask adds a layer mask that shows only the selection. Transform starts Free Transform on the selected part.

Crop and Slice tools

Crop trims the image. Drag to draw the crop area, or tap for the whole image. Drag a corner or edge to resize it (drag past the image to add canvas), and drag inside to move it. Parts outside are dimmed. Tap Commit to crop or Cancel to stop. Options: Straighten (draw a line along something that should be level, and the crop turns to match), Delete Cropped Pixels (off keeps the cut-away parts hidden, so you can move them back in later).

Perspective Crop crops and squares up something photographed at an angle, such as a sign or a page. Tap its four corners (drag one to move it), then tap Commit. Options while placing: Commit, Cancel, Undo Last Point.

Slice cuts the image into slices for the web. Drag a box to make a slice.

Slice Select shows the name and number of the slice you tap.

Frame

Frame makes a picture frame layer where you drag a box.

Measuring tools

Eyedropper picks the foreground colour from the image. Tap, or drag to see the colour change as you go. Options: Sample Size (Point Sample, or an average of 3 by 3 up to 101 by 101 pixels), Sample (All Layers, Current Layer, Current & Below, and the same two without adjustment layers).

Color Sampler marks a spot so you can read its colour. Tap to add a sampler; a message shows its number and its R, G and B values. Drag a sampler to move it.

Ruler measures distance and angle. Drag a line; the options bar shows its width (W), height (H), length (L) in pixels and angle (A). Drag either end to change it.

Note adds a note to the image. Tap where you want it and type your note.

Count puts numbered count marks on the image. Tap each thing you want to count; a message shows the total.

Repair tools

Spot Repair Brush fixes small spots and blemishes. Paint over them and they’re filled to blend with what’s around. Options: Size, Hardness, Sample All Layers, Straight Line (see below).

Quick Fill removes something by filling it from the surroundings, with no AI. Paint over it and lift. Options: Size, Sample All Layers, Remove After Each Stroke (on: each stroke is filled when you lift; off: paint several strokes, then tap Commit to fill them all at once), Straight Line.

AI Remove removes things with an AI model that fills the gap with what should be behind it. It runs on your device, so your photos never leave it. Paint over what you want gone, or select it and tap Remove Selection. Options: Size, Sample All Layers, Remove After Each Stroke (as for Quick Fill), Match Colour (blends the fill into the colour round it), Remove Selection (shown when something is selected), Straight Line.

The first time you use AI Remove, PhotoWagon asks to download its model (about 25 MB, once). Tap Download to get it, or Not Now. When the download finishes, what you painted or selected is removed.

Repair Brush copies texture from one place to cover another, matched to the light and colour where you paint. Touch and hold to set the source, then paint over the problem. Options: Size, Opacity, Hardness, Straight Line.

Patch repairs a selected area with texture from somewhere else. Select the area first (with any selection tool), switch to Patch, then drag from the selection to a clean area to take the patch from.

Move and Fill moves a selected part of the image and fills the hole it leaves. Select it, switch to Move and Fill, then drag it to where you want it.

Red Eye fixes red eyes from a camera flash. Tap each red eye.

Brush tools

Brush paints with the foreground colour. Paint with your finger or Apple Pencil; pressure works with the Pencil.

Options: Brush Tip (pick a brush preset from its groups, or Import Brush Tips…), Mode (how the paint blends with what’s below, such as Normal, Multiply or Screen), Size, Opacity, Hardness, Flow (how much paint each dab lays down), Smoothing (evens out shaky strokes), Pressure for Size, Pressure for Opacity (the Pencil’s pressure changes size or opacity), Symmetry (Off, Vertical, Horizontal, Dual Axis, Diagonal: paints a mirror image across the middle of the canvas), Brush Angle (the angle dial), Brush Settings (the full brush screen), Straight Line.

On a narrow screen only the sliders that fit are shown, and the rest move into the Brush Options (…) menu: Mode, the two pressure buttons, Symmetry, Straight Line, Brush Angle… and Brush Settings….

Straight Line. With Straight Line on (or Shift held on a keyboard), a tap paints a straight line from where your last stroke ended to where you tap. It works with every painting and retouching tool.

Pencil paints hard-edged lines. Options: as for Brush, without Hardness and Flow.

Color Replacement paints the foreground colour over the colours you paint on, keeping their light and shade. Options: Size, Straight Line.

Mixer Brush paints colour that mixes with the colours already there, like wet paint. Options: Size, Flow, Smoothing, Pressure for Size, Pressure for Opacity, Brush Angle, Brush Settings, Straight Line.

Brush Angle

The Brush Angle dial shows the brush tip’s shape at its angle. Drag round the dial to turn it, or tap the turn buttons to move it 15° either way. Reset sets it back to 0°.

Brush Settings

Brush Settings opens a screen with every setting of the brush, with a preview stroke at the top that updates as you change things. Tap Done when you’re finished.

Brush Tip Shape. Size, Hardness, Angle, Roundness (squashes the tip into an oval), Flip X, Flip Y, Spacing (on: sets the gap between dabs).

Shape Dynamics (turn on with its switch). Size Jitter with Minimum Diameter, Tilt Scale, Angle Jitter, Roundness Jitter with Minimum Roundness, Flip X Jitter, Flip Y Jitter. Each jitter adds random change as you paint.

Scattering (turn on with its switch). Scatter (spreads dabs away from the stroke), Both Axes, Count (dabs per step), Count Jitter.

Transfer (turn on with its switch). Opacity Jitter and Flow Jitter, each with a Minimum.

Each jitter has a Control that sets what drives it: Off, Fade, Pen Pressure, Pen Tilt, Pencil Rotation, Initial Direction or Direction.

Flow and Smoothing. Flow, Smoothing, Pulled String Mode (the stroke only moves once your finger pulls past a short string), Stroke Catch-up, Catch-up on Stroke End, Adjust for Zoom.

Other settings. Noise (adds grain to soft edges), Wet Edges (darker edges, like watercolour), Build-up (paint keeps building while you hold).

New Brush Preset… saves the brush under a name you give, so it shows up in the Brush Tip menu. Include Tool Settings also saves the mode, opacity, flow and the pressure buttons with it.

Stamp tools

Clone Stamp paints a copy of one part of the image onto another. Touch and hold to set the source, then paint; if no source is set yet, PhotoWagon reminds you. Options: as for Brush, without Brush Tip, Mode and Symmetry.

Pattern Stamp paints with a pattern. Options: as for Clone Stamp.

History Brush

History Brush paints part of the image back to how it was earlier, so you can undo an edit in one area only. Options: as for Clone Stamp.

Eraser tools

Eraser erases to transparent as you paint. Options: as for Brush, without Mode.

Background Eraser erases the colour you paint over while leaving other colours, handy for removing a plain background round an edge. Options: Size, Hardness, Straight Line.

Magic Eraser erases all colours like the one you tap. Options: Tolerance, Contiguous, Sample All Layers.

Gradient and Paint Bucket

Gradient fills the layer with a smooth blend of colours. Drag from where it starts to where it ends. Options: Gradient (pick a gradient preset; the default is Foreground to Background), the style (Linear, Radial, Angle, Reflected, Diamond), Reverse (swaps the ends).

Paint Bucket fills colours like the one you tap with the foreground colour. Options: Tolerance, Contiguous, Sample All Layers.

Blur, Sharpen and Smudge

Blur softens detail where you paint. Options: Size, Strength, Hardness, Straight Line.

Sharpen makes detail crisper where you paint. Options: as for Blur.

Smudge smears colour along your stroke, like a finger in wet paint. Options: as for Blur.

Dodge, Burn and Sponge

Dodge lightens where you paint. Options: Size, Exposure (how strong each stroke is), Hardness, Straight Line.

Burn darkens where you paint. Options: as for Dodge.

Sponge takes colour out where you paint. Options: Size, Flow, Hardness, Straight Line.

Pen tools

The pen tools draw a path, a smooth outline you can turn into a selection, a fill, a stroke or a shape. All of them share these options: Make Selection (selects the inside of the path, using the selection mode), Fill Path (fills it with the foreground colour), Stroke Path (paints along it with the brush), Shape Layer (makes it a new shape layer), Delete Path.

Pen draws a path point by point. Tap for a corner, or drag to pull out handles for a curve. Tap the first point to close the shape.

Freeform Pen draws a path as you move your finger, like drawing with a pencil.

Curvature Pen makes smooth curves. Tap points and the path curves through all of them.

Add Anchor Point adds a point where you tap on a path.

Delete Anchor Point removes the point you tap.

Convert Point changes the point you tap from a corner to a smooth curve, or back.

Type tools

Horizontal Type adds text. Tap for a line of text, or drag a box for text that wraps inside it. Type your text and confirm. Tap existing text to edit it. The text uses the foreground colour. Options: Font, Size (in points), Leading (the gap between lines; empty is Auto), Tracking (the space between letters), and left, centre or right alignment. With a text layer active, changes apply to it straight away.

Vertical Type adds text that runs top to bottom. Options: as for Horizontal Type.

Horizontal Type Mask and Vertical Type Mask make a selection in the shape of the letters you type, instead of a text layer. Options: as for Horizontal Type.

Path selection tools

Path Selection moves a whole path. Drag it where you want it.

Direct Selection changes a path’s shape. Drag a point to move it, or drag a handle to change the curve. Options for both: as for the pen tools.

Shape tools

Each shape tool draws a new shape layer filled with the foreground colour. Drag to draw the shape; hold Shift on a keyboard for an even square or circle.

Rectangle draws a rectangle. Ellipse draws an oval. Triangle draws a triangle.

Polygon draws a shape with straight sides. Options: Sides (3 to 100).

Line draws a straight line from where you start dragging to where you stop. Its thickness depends on how far you’re zoomed in.

Custom Shape draws a ready-made shape from the shape presets.

Fluid Warp

Fluid Warp (☰ › Filter › Fluid Warp…) lets you push and pull the pixels of a layer by painting on the image. Drag to warp; two fingers still zoom and pan. Tap Commit to apply or Cancel to stop. Options: the warp tool (Forward Warp pushes pixels along, Reconstruct paints back towards the original, Smooth, Twirl, Pucker pulls in, Bloat pushes out), Size, Pressure, and under More, Amount (how much of the warp shows, 0 to 100%) and Restore All (removes every warp stroke).

File, Edit, View and Window

Tap ☰ at the top left to open the menus. This section goes through File, Edit, View and Window from top to bottom, in the order you see them. A greyed-out item tells you, in small print under its name, what it needs first.

At the very top of ☰, above the menus, Quick Share as PNG… opens the share sheet with a PNG of your image, ready to send.

Items whose names end in “…” open a settings sheet first. Change what you need, then tap OK to run it, or Cancel to leave things as they were.

File

New, Open and your recent files are on Home (the house at the top left).

New… (Home › New file) makes a blank document. Pick a Preset (Photo, Print, Art & Illustration, Web, Mobile, Film & Video) or set the size yourself. Options: Name, Units (Pixels, Inches, Centimeters), Width, Height, Resolution (pixels/inch), Colour Mode (RGB Color, Grayscale, CMYK Color, Lab Color), Colour Profile, Bit Depth (8, 16 or 32 bit), Background (White, Black, Background Color, Transparent). Tap Create.

Open (on Home) lists the pictures in On My iPhone › PhotoWagon, newest first. Tap one to open it. Browse Files… lets you pick from anywhere in Files, and Photos… from your photo library. You can open JPEG, PNG, HEIC, JPEG XL, TIFF, PSD, WebP, GIF, BMP, SVG, EXR, camera raw files and more. A JPEG XL file made of layers opens with its layers, and an animated one with a layer per frame.

Photos (on Home) goes straight to your photo library and opens the picture you pick as a new document.

Recent (on Home) lists the files you’ve opened or saved lately (up to 20). If a file has moved or been deleted, the app tells you it can’t be found.

Close closes the document in front. If it has changes you haven’t saved, you’re asked first: Save, Don’t Save or Cancel.

Save writes your changes back into the document’s own file. If the document is new, or its file can’t hold layers (a JPEG with several layers, say), Save As opens instead.

Save As… saves the document as a new file, and from then on you’re working in that file. Options: Name, Format, Where (On My iPhone › PhotoWagon, or Other Location… to choose a folder). The formats that keep layers are PhotoCraft (the default), PSD and TIFF. A one-layer document can also go out as JPEG, PNG, WebP, JPEG XL, GIF or BMP. JPEG adds a Quality slider from 0 to 12. JPEG XL adds Lossless (on: every pixel kept exactly, at its full depth), Quality from 1 to 100 when Lossless is off, and Effort from 1 to 10 (higher takes longer and makes a smaller file); its alpha channels go into the file too.

These three menus live under ☰ in the top bar. Image changes the whole picture (its size, colours and colour mode), Select makes and reshapes selections, and Type works on text layers.

Image

Desaturate removes all colour from the layer and leaves the greys. It runs straight away.

Options: Preset (Default, Brighter, Darker, More Contrast, Strong Contrast, Lift Shadows, Faded, Negative), Auto and Options…, Channel, Show Clipping, points or pencil (draw the curve with your finger, then Smooth it), a grid size button, Input and Output for the chosen point, a bin to delete it, and the same three eyedroppers as Levels.

Selective Color… changes how much cyan, magenta, yellow and black goes into one range of colours, such as only the reds or only the neutrals. Options: Colors (the range to change), Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black, Method (Relative changes by a share of what’s there, Absolute by a fixed amount).

Shadows/Highlights… brings back detail in dark shadows and bright highlights without changing the middle tones much. Good for backlit photos. Options: Shadow amount, Shadow tone, Highlight amount, Highlight tone, Radius (the size of the area each pixel is compared with), Color (saturation), Midtone (contrast), Black clip, White clip.

Layers

A picture in PhotoWagon is a stack of layers, like sheets of clear film laid on top of each other. What’s on a higher layer covers what’s below it, unless you fade it, blend it or mask parts of it away. Keeping things on their own layers means you can change one part later without touching the rest.

Masks

A mask hides parts of a layer without erasing them. Where the mask is white the layer shows, where it’s black the layer is hidden, and grey is in between. Paint on the mask in black to hide, in white to bring back. Because nothing is deleted, you can always change your mind.

Adding a mask. Tap Add Mask in the Layers panel, or use ☰ › Layer › Layer Mask. With a selection, the new mask shows only the selected area; touch and hold the button to hide the selected area instead.

Painting on the mask. Tap the mask thumbnail in the layer’s row. It gets a coloured outline, and painting, filling and filters now work on the mask. Tap the layer thumbnail to go back to the pixels.

The mask’s touch-and-hold menu. Touch and hold the mask thumbnail for these:

Unlink Mask lets you move the layer and its mask on their own; Link Mask joins them again.

Invert Mask swaps what’s hidden and shown.

Load as Selection turns the mask into a selection.

Copy Mask To and Move Mask To put this mask on another layer you pick.

Apply Mask (pixel layers) makes the hiding permanent and removes the mask.

Delete Mask removes it.

Mask Properties. Double-tap the mask thumbnail, or open ☰ › Window › Properties while the mask is selected. Density fades the whole mask, so hidden parts show through a little. Feather softens the mask’s edges, up to 1000 pixels. Neither changes what you painted, so you can adjust them later. Below are Invert, Disable Mask, Unlink from Layer, Apply Mask and Delete Mask.

Vector masks hide a layer using a path instead of pixels, so their edges stay sharp at any size. Add and remove them from ☰ › Layer › Vector Mask. They don’t show a thumbnail in the Layers panel.

Clipping masks are another way to hide: a clipped layer only shows where the layer below it has pixels. It’s handy for putting a photo inside text, or for making an adjustment affect only one layer. Use Create Clipping Mask from a layer’s touch-and-hold menu or the Layer menu.

Embedded layers and live filters

An embedded layer holds other layers, or a whole picture, inside it. You can scale, rotate and filter it as often as you like without losing quality, because PhotoWagon always works from the original inside. Embedded layers have a small badge on their thumbnail.

To make one, choose Convert to Embedded Layer from a layer’s touch-and-hold menu, or select several layers and tap Embedded Layer. ☰ › File › Place Embedded… and Place from Photos… also add pictures as embedded layers.

Filters and Develop

Filters change the pixels of a layer: blur it, sharpen it, add noise, bend it or paint it in a new style. You’ll find them all under ☰ › Filter. Develop is a full photo-finishing screen for light, colour and detail, and it’s also where camera raw files open.

How filter settings work

Running a filter. Pick the layer you want to change in the Layers panel, then choose a filter from ☰ › Filter. If there’s a selection, only the selected part changes. Items without “…” at the end run straight away with no questions.

The settings sheet. Items ending in “…” open their settings first. On an iPhone they open in a sheet with a small preview of the image at the top. On an iPad they open in a column beside the canvas, so you see the change on the full image. Each number has a slider and a box you can type in; choices are lists and switches are toggles.

Trying settings. The filter is tried each time you let go of a slider, enter a number or pick a choice. A spinner shows while it works. Tap OK to keep the result, or Cancel to put the image back as it was. Swiping the sheet away is turned off, so you never keep a try by accident.

Preview. The eye button at the top left of the sheet turns the preview off and on. Off shows the image without the filter, so you can compare before and after without losing your settings.

Handles on the preview. Tilt-Shift, Iris Blur, Spin Blur, Path Blur and Lighting Effects show handles on the image. Drag them to place the centre, the edges, the ends or the light. The other settings sit in the form below.

Last Filter. ☰ › Filter › Last Filter runs the most recent filter again, with the same settings, on the layer you have selected now. With a keyboard, press ⌘⌥F.

Perspective Planes… Meant for pasting and cloning along a surface in perspective. Its sheet only has Focal length and New layer so far, with no way to draw a plane, so it can’t do anything useful yet.

Fluid Warp… Push, pull, twist and swell parts of a layer by painting on the canvas. Pick the tool in the options bar, then drag on the image; two fingers still zoom and pan. Tap ✓ (Commit) to keep the warp or ✕ (Cancel) to drop it. With a keyboard, press ⌘⇧X.

Fluid Warp’s options bar: Tool (Forward Warp pushes pixels along your stroke, Reconstruct paints them back, Smooth evens out the warp, Twirl turns them clockwise, Pucker pulls them in, Bloat pushes them out), Size (10 to 1000 pixels), Pressure (how strong each stroke is). Under More: Amount (how much of the warp shows, from 100% down to 0%) and Restore All (start again).

Blur

Gaussian Blur… A smooth, even blur. Good for softening a background or a mask. Options: Radius (how far the blur spreads, in pixels).

Blur, Blur More. A light blur with no settings. Blur More is a few times stronger.

Box Blur… Blurs with square averaging, which looks a little blocky in highlights. Options: Radius.

Motion Blur… Streaks the image in one direction, as if it moved. Options: Angle (the direction), Distance (how long the streaks are).

Radial Blur… Blurs in circles round a point, or out from it like a zoom. Options: Amount, Method (Spin or Zoom), Quality (Draft, Good or Best), Center x and Center y (where the centre is, 0 to 1 across and down the image).

Surface Blur… Smooths flat areas but keeps edges sharp. Handy for skin and noise. Options: Radius, Threshold (how different pixels must be to count as an edge).

Smart Blur… Blurs inside areas of similar colour and can show the edges it finds. Options: Radius, Threshold, Quality (High, Medium or Low), Mode (Normal, Edge only, Overlay edge).

Lens Blur… Imitates a camera’s out-of-focus blur, with shaped highlights. Options: Radius, Shape (Hexagon, Triangle, Square, Pentagon, Heptagon or Octagon), Blade curvature, Rotation, Depth map (None, Transparency or Layer mask), Focal distance, Invert, Brightness and Threshold (for bright spots), Noise, Distribution (Uniform or Gaussian), Monochromatic.

Shape Blur… Blurs using a shape, which shows in bright spots. Options: Radius, Shape (Circle, Ring, Square, Diamond, Triangle, Hexagon, Star, Heart or Cross).

Average. Fills the layer, or the selection, with its average colour. No settings. Useful for finding the overall colour of an area.

Sharpen

Sharpen, Sharpen More. Quick sharpening with no settings. Sharpen More is stronger.

Sharpen Edges. Sharpens only the edges and leaves smooth areas alone. No settings.

Unsharp Mask… Classic, controllable sharpening. Options: Amount (how strong, in percent), Radius (how wide the sharpened edges are), Threshold (leave areas this smooth alone, so noise isn’t sharpened).

Smart Sharpen… Sharpening that also holds back noise. Options: Amount, Radius, Reduce noise.

Noise

Despeckle. Removes fine specks and grain while keeping edges. No settings.

Add Noise… Adds grain, for a film look or to hide banding. Options: Amount, Distribution (Uniform or Gaussian), Monochromatic (grey grain instead of coloured).

Median… Replaces each pixel with the middle value of its neighbours, which wipes out specks. Options: Radius.

Dust & Scratches… Removes small marks that stand out from their surroundings. Options: Radius (how big a mark can be), Threshold (how different it must be).

Reduce Noise… Cleans up grainy photos. Options: Strength, Preserve details, Reduce color noise, Sharpen details, Remove jpeg artifact (smooths the blocks of heavily squeezed JPEGs).

Pixelate

Mosaic… Turns the image into square blocks. Options: Cell size.

Color Halftone… Turns the image into printed-looking dots, one screen per colour. Options: Max radius (the biggest dot), Channel1 to Channel4 (the angle of each colour’s dots).

Crystallize… Breaks the image into flat, crystal-shaped cells. Options: Cell size.

Facet. Groups similar pixels into flat patches, a little like a painting. No settings.

Fragment. Overlays four offset copies for a shaky, doubled look. No settings.

Mezzotint… Turns the image into a pattern of dots, lines or strokes. Options: Type (Fine dots, Medium dots, Grainy dots, Coarse dots, Short lines, Medium lines, Long lines, Short strokes, Medium strokes or Long strokes).

Pointillize… Paints the image in dots of colour over the background colour. Options: Cell size.

Stylize

Emboss… Makes the image look raised or stamped into grey metal. Options: Angle (where the light comes from), Height, Amount.

Find Edges. Outlines the edges in the image on white. No settings.

Solarize. Blends a negative with the positive, like a photo exposed to light while developing. No settings.

Diffuse… Scatters pixels for a soft, frosted look. Options: Mode (Normal, Darken only, Lighten only or Anisotropic).

Extrude… Pushes the image out into 3D blocks or pyramids. Options: Type (Blocks or Pyramids), Size, Depth, Depth mode (Random or Level based), Solid front faces, Mask incomplete blocks.

Oil Paint… Makes a photo look painted with thick brush strokes. Options: Stylization, Cleanliness, Scale, Bristle detail, Lighting (shows the paint’s ridges), Angle (of the light), Shine.

Tiles… Cuts the image into tiles and nudges them apart. Options: Count (tiles across), Max offset, Fill (what shows in the gaps: Background, Foreground, Inverse or Unaltered).

Trace Contour… Draws thin lines where brightness crosses a level, like a contour map. Options: Level, Edge (Lower or Upper).

Wind… Adds streaks as if wind blew across the image. Options: Method (Wind, Blast or Stagger), Direction (From right or From left).

Distort

Twirl… Spins the image round its centre, more in the middle than at the edges. Options: Angle.

Pinch… Squeezes the middle in, or pushes it out with a negative amount. Options: Amount.

Spherize… Wraps the image round a ball, for a bulging look. Options: Amount, Mode (Normal, Horizontal only or Vertical only).

Wave… Ripples the image with mixed waves. Options: Generators (how many waves), Wavelength min and Wavelength max, Amplitude min and Amplitude max, Type (Sine, Triangle or Square), Undefined areas (Wrap or Repeat).

Ripple… Small ripples like the surface of water. Options: Amount, Size (Small, Medium or Large).

Polar Coordinates… Bends a strip into a circle, or opens a circle out into a strip. Options: Mode (Rectangular to polar or Polar to rectangular).

Displace… Moves pixels by the brightness of another image (the map), for example to make a picture follow the folds of cloth. Open the map as a document first. Options: Displacement Map (which open document), Horizontal and Vertical (how far), Fit (Stretch or Tile), Undefined areas (Repeat or Wrap).

Shear… Bends the image sideways along a curve. Drag the line in the graph sideways and tap it to add a point; Reset makes it straight again. Options: Undefined Areas (Wrap Around or Repeat Edge Pixels).

ZigZag… Ripples out from the centre, like a stone dropped in a pond. Options: Amount, Ridges, Style (Pond ripples, Out from center or Around center).

Other

High Pass… Keeps only edges and fine detail, greying the rest. Often set to Overlay for sharpening. Options: Radius.

Minimum…, Maximum… Minimum grows the dark areas and Maximum grows the light ones. They’re useful for shrinking or spreading a mask. Options: Radius, Preserve (Squareness or Roundness).

Offset… Slides the image across, by a number of pixels. Options: Horizontal, Vertical, Undefined areas (what fills the gap: Wrap, Repeat or Transparent).

Custom… Applies your own sharpening or blurring pattern. Its sheet only shows Scale and Offset, with no grid to enter the pattern, so it isn’t much use yet.

HSB/HSL. Swaps how the colour is stored in the pixels, from RGB to hue, saturation and brightness. It runs straight away with no settings, and the result looks strange on its own.

Color to Alpha… Turns a colour into transparency, for example to lift a drawing off white paper. Soft edges stay soft. Options: Color (the colour to remove), Transparency threshold, Opacity threshold.

Render

Fibers… Draws a woven fibre texture in your foreground and background colours. Options: Variance (how much the colour changes), Strength (how long the fibres are).

Lens Flare… Adds the bright glare a camera lens catches from a light. Options: Brightness, Center x and Center y (where the flare is), Lens (Zoom, Prime35, Prime105 or Movie prime).

Lighting Effects… Shines a light on the image. Drag the yellow handle for the light and the white one for where it points. Options: Light type (Spot, Point or Infinite), Intensity, Light z (height), Cone, Hotspot, Angle, Elevation, Gloss, Metallic, Exposure, Ambience, Texture (None, Red, Green, Blue, Alpha or Luminance, for bumps), Height, White is high.

Relight… Changes where the light seems to fall on a photo, keeping its detail. Options: Angle and Elevation (where the new light comes from), Intensity, Ambient (light from everywhere), Warmth, Softness.

Clouds, Difference Clouds. Clouds fills the layer with soft clouds in your foreground and background colours. Difference Clouds blends clouds into what’s there, flipping colours. No settings.

Picture Frame… Draws a decorative frame round the image. Options: Frame (Vine with flowers, Vine with leaves, Ivy, Roses, Daisies, Berries, Bamboo, Waves, Zigzag, Dots, Rope, Scallops, Double line, Snowflakes, Stars or Hearts), Margin, Size, Arrangement, Lines, Thickness, Fade, Vine color, Flower color, Leaf color, New layer.

Tree… Draws a tree. Options: Base tree type (1 to 34), Light direction (1 to 5), Leaves amount, Leaves size, Branches height, Branches thickness, Default leaves, Leaves color, Custom branch color, Branches color, Flat shading, X and Y (where it stands), Size, New layer.

Blur Effects

Tilt-Shift… Keeps a band sharp and blurs above and below it, so a scene looks like a model. Drag the handle to move the band. Options: Blur, Angle, Focus (how wide the sharp band is), Transition (how gently it fades).

Iris Blur… Keeps an oval sharp and blurs round it. Drag the centre handle to move it and the two edge handles to size it. Options: Blur, Angle, Roundness, Feather.

Variable Blur… Blurs more where the selection is stronger, so you can paint where the blur goes. Options: Radius, Mask (the name of a saved selection to use instead).

Spin Blur… Spins an oval area, like a turning wheel. Drag the handles to place and size it. Options: Blur angle (how far it spins), Angle.

Path Blur… Blurs along a line, as if the subject moved. Drag the start and end handles. Options: Speed.

Video

De-Interlace… Removes the comb-like lines from a frame grabbed from video. Options: Eliminate (Odd fields or Even fields), Create by (Interpolation or Duplication).

NTSC Colors. Tones colours down to what old TV broadcasts can show. No settings.

Plug-ins

Install Plug-in… Asks for a Path to a plug-in file and has a Replace switch. On iPhone and iPad there’s no way to pick the file yet.

Develop

Develop is a full screen for finishing photos: light, colour, curves, detail, presets and snapshots, with the image at the top. Open it from ☰ › Filter › Develop… to work on the selected layer.

Opening files in Develop. Camera raw files (such as DNG, CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, ORF, RW2 and PEF) always open in Develop first. To open JPEG and HEIC photos there too, turn on Open JPEG and HEIC in Develop in ☰ › Edit › Preferences › General…, under File Handling.

Open, Open as Object, Done. When a file opens in Develop, tap Open to open it with your settings applied. Hold Open for Open as Object, which opens it as an embedded layer with Develop as a live filter you can change later. Done, in the ⋯ menu, keeps your settings for the next time you open that file, and closes it without opening. Cancel closes it.

Workflow. Shown only while opening a file, and applied when you tap Open. Options: Color Space (As Opened, sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB (1998) compatible or ProPhoto RGB compatible), Depth (As Opened, 8 Bits/Channel or 16 Bits/Channel).

OK and Cancel. When Develop is opened from the Filter menu or from a live filter, OK keeps the result and Cancel puts things back.

Trying settings. The image updates each time you let go of a control. The eye button at the top left shows the image before and after your settings.

Undo, Redo, Reset to Default. Under the ⋯ button at the top right. Undo and Redo step through your changes in Develop. Reset to Default sets everything back but keeps your snapshots.

Profile. Color or Monochrome (black and white).

Auto. Sets the Light sliders for you from the image’s brightness. Change them after if you like.

Show Clipping. Outlines the parts of the image that are pure black (in cyan) or pure white (in red).

Sliders. Double-tap a slider’s name to reset it. Hold the name for Reset and, where there is one, Auto for that slider alone.

Panels. The settings are grouped in panels. Tap a panel’s name to open or close it, and tap its eye to turn its settings off and on, to compare.

Light. Brightness and contrast. Options: Exposure (overall brightness, in stops), Contrast, Highlights (bright areas), Shadows (dark areas), Whites (the very brightest), Blacks (the very darkest).

Color. White balance and colour strength. Options: White Balance (As Shot, Auto or Custom), White Balance Tool (hold on the image, then let go on something that should be grey), Temperature (cooler or warmer), Tint (green or magenta), Vibrance (boosts dull colours more than strong ones), Saturation.

Effects. Finishing touches. Options: Texture (fine detail), Clarity (mid-size contrast), Dehaze (cuts or adds haze), Vignette (darkens or lightens the corners) with Midpoint, Roundness and Feather, Grain with Size and Roughness.

Curve. A tone curve over the image’s histogram. Pick RGB, Red, Green or Blue, then tap the curve to add a point and drag points to move them. Drag a point off the graph to remove it.

Color Mixer. Changes one range of colours at a time. Pick Hue, Saturation or Luminance, then move the slider for Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Aqua, Blue, Purple or Magenta.

Detail. Sharpening and noise. Options: Sharpening with Radius, Detail and Masking (sharpen only the edges), Noise Reduction with Detail, Color Noise Reduction with Detail.

Presets. One tap applies a look: Bright, Warm Matte, Punchy or Black & White, plus any you’ve saved. After you pick one, Amount (0 to 200%) makes it weaker or stronger. Save Preset… saves your current settings under a name.

Snapshots. Save Snapshot… saves the current settings under a name, so you can try other ideas and come back. Tap a snapshot to go back to it. Snapshots are kept with the image’s Develop settings.
